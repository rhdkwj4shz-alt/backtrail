Privacy

Last updated 10 October 2026.

Backtrail collects no data about you. There's no account, no advertising, no analytics and no tracking. Nothing you put into Backtrail is sent to the developer.

What stays on your devices

  • Your location, recorded routes, waypoints and trip notes.
  • Your Medical ID, emergency contacts and emergency plans.
  • Your incident log and Kids' Photos. These stay on your iPhone only and aren't in any backup. Kids' Photos are deleted after a day.
  • Your settings, activities and safety check-up.
  • Where a hard fall or a fall into water happened, marked on the walk you were recording. Backtrail uses this only to help you, and it goes wherever that walk goes.

Your iPhone and Apple Watch share these with each other directly. Like any app's data, most of them are also in your iPhone's own backups to iCloud or a computer, if you use those. Otherwise they go nowhere unless you turn on Backtrail's iCloud Backup.

iCloud Backup, if you turn it on

iCloud Backup is off until you turn it on in Me, then iCloud Backup. It stores your plans, routes, Medical ID, contacts and settings in your own iCloud account, end-to-end encrypted with keys kept in your iCloud Keychain. Neither the developer nor Apple can read them. Apple's privacy policy covers iCloud.

A walk or plan you delete in Backtrail leaves the backup the next time Backtrail backs up. Turn iCloud Backup off to stop backing up; your last backup stays in iCloud until you delete it. To remove it, tap Delete Backup from iCloud in Me, then iCloud Backup, or delete Backtrail's data in Settings, then your name, then iCloud, then Manage Account Storage. Deleting the app doesn't remove the backup.

What leaves your devices, and only when you use it

  • Topo map tiles. To show and save maps, Backtrail downloads map tiles from Ordnance Survey (Great Britain), the US Geological Survey, Land Information New Zealand, NSW Spatial Services and Geoscience Australia. Like any website, those services see your device's internet address and which map squares were requested, which shows roughly where you were looking. Backtrail sends them nothing else. Apple's map, when you use it, is provided by Apple under Apple's privacy policy.
  • Find Each Other. While you're in a group, your name and position go straight to the other iPhones in it nearby, encrypted, phone to phone, with no server or internet. Only phones that joined with your group's code, from a QR code or AirDrop, can read them. Everyone in the group sees your name.
  • Tide times. For US coasts, Backtrail picks the nearest tide station on your iPhone, then asks NOAA, the US government's tide service, for that station's predictions. NOAA sees your device's internet address and which station, which shows roughly where you are, and nothing else.
  • Weather. When you plan a walk or check its forecast, Backtrail asks Apple Weather for the forecast where the walk starts, which sends that location to Apple. Apple's privacy policy covers it, and nothing goes to the developer.
  • Messages and calls. When you tell someone your plan or call for help, the text or call goes through your iPhone's Messages and Phone apps, to the people and numbers you choose.
  • Health. If you turn on Save Walks to Health, finished walks are saved in the Health app as Hiking workouts with their route and heart rate, and Apple's Health privacy applies. While one of these walks records on Apple Watch, Backtrail reads your heart rate to show it on your Watch and iPhone. It reads nothing else from Health and doesn't send your heart rate anywhere.
  • Siri and Spotlight. Your guides, waypoints, routes and plans are listed in Spotlight on your iPhone, and that index stays on your iPhone. When you ask Siri about them, Apple handles the request under Apple's privacy policy. Nothing goes to the developer.
  • Translation. Help in Another Language translates your phrases and Medical ID on your iPhone with Apple's Translation. The first time you use a language, your iPhone downloads it from Apple; your words aren't sent anywhere.
  • Apple Intelligence. The pack check runs on your iPhone with Apple's on-device model. Your trip details don't leave your iPhone for it.
  • Crash reports. If you choose to share analytics with app developers in your iPhone's settings, Apple may pass anonymous crash reports to the developer.

Children

Backtrail doesn't knowingly collect information from anyone, including children.
